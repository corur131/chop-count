# Chop Count trainer: evidence base (Oct 2026)

## Goals and rates
- Fat loss: 0.5–1.0% of body weight per week is the evidence-based range for keeping muscle (Helms et al. / Nutrients 2021 fat-loss review; Precision Nutrition). Leaner people should use the slow end.
- Lean gain: novice/intermediate 0.25–0.5% of body weight per week, surplus about 10–20% (Iraki et al. 2019, Sports 7:154). Advanced slower.
- Expected muscle gain per month (Precision Nutrition): men beginner 0.7–1.1 kg, intermediate 0.35–0.55, advanced 0.17–0.28; women beginner 0.3–0.45 kg, intermediate 0.15–0.23, advanced 0.07–0.11.
- Protein: 1.6–2.2 g/kg (Iraki 2019; ISSN). For people carrying extra fat, base protein on a reference weight (goal weight / BMI 25 weight), not current weight.

## Body shape
- Spot reduction does not work: a 2021 meta-analysis (13 studies, >1,100 people) found training an area does not remove fat from it (Ramírez-Campillo et al.; University of Sydney summary). Hips and waist shrink only through overall fat loss.
- Hip bone width can't be changed by training; hip dips are bone plus glute medius plus fat (Standard Media summary of clinicians). Large glute medius volume adds width at the side of the hips.
- Hip thrust vs squat (Plotkin et al. 2023, Frontiers in Physiology, MRI, 9 weeks): similar glute max growth; squats grew quads and adductors more; hip thrust is more glute-specific. Glute medius/minimus barely grew from either. EMG alone does not predict growth.
- Glute programming (E3 Rehab summary): mix long-length moves (squat, split squat, RDL, step-up) with short-length moves (hip thrust, bridge, kickback); 2–3 sessions per week; 6–20 reps; near failure; progressive overload.

## Training variables
- Volume: more weekly sets per muscle gives more growth with diminishing returns (Pelland et al. 2024). Rough bands: under 10 low, 10–20 moderate, over 20 high.
- Frequency: train each muscle at least twice per week.
- Proximity to failure: hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure; strength depends little on it (Robinson et al. 2023 meta-regression).
- Deloads: pre-planned every 4–8 weeks, 5–7 days, cut volume 25–60% and/or add 1–3 RIR (Bell et al. 2023).

## Men vs women
- Relative muscle growth is essentially the same; women gain relatively more upper-body strength (Roberts 2020; Refalo 2025). No evidence women need fundamentally different programs; program by goal and experience. Differences come from goals (e.g. glute priority vs upper-body size).
- No good evidence to adapt training to menstrual-cycle phase (Colenso-Semple et al. 2023 umbrella review); track symptoms if they affect training.

## Steps
- Adaptive goal: rank the last 9 days of steps; the goal is the 60th percentile; updated daily. In an RCT it raised steps +2,205/day (48%) vs +984 (18%) for a fixed 10,000 goal, and goals were met on 58% of days vs 22% (Adams et al. 2013, PLOS ONE).
- Mortality benefit plateaus around 8,000–10,000 steps/day under 60, 6,000–8,000 at 60+ (Paluch et al. 2022, Lancet Public Health).

## Safety
- PAR-Q screening (7 questions) before starting; any "yes" means talk to a doctor first.
